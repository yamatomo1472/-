## 📖 Word of the Day

**Tariff**

*Noun | /ˈtær.ɪf/ | Origin: From Arabic "ta'rif," meaning notification or list of fees, which passed into Spanish and Italian before entering English in the 16th century.*

**Definition:** A tax that a government places on goods imported from another country. Tariffs make foreign products more expensive, which can protect domestic industries but also raise prices for consumers.

**In context:**
1. "The United States imposed a 25 percent tariff on Japanese automobiles, raising the price of imported cars for American buyers."
2. "Japan's trade negotiators are working to reduce tariffs on steel and electronics before the new fiscal year begins."
3. "Our export team needs to recalculate pricing models because the new tariff structure changes our margins significantly in the U.S. market."

**Why it matters:** The word "tariff" appears constantly in international business news, trade negotiations, and financial reports. Understanding its meaning — and the difference between a tariff, a quota, and a trade agreement — helps you follow economic news accurately and participate confidently in business discussions about pricing, supply chains, and market strategy.
