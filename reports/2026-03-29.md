**Headwind**

*Noun | /ˈhɛd.wɪnd/ | Origin: from "head" (front-facing) + "wind" — originally a sailing and aviation term for wind blowing directly against a vessel's forward movement*

**Definition:** A headwind is a force or condition that makes progress more difficult. In business and economics, it refers to any factor — such as rising costs, weak demand, or trade barriers — that slows growth or makes it harder to achieve goals.

**In context:**
1. "Toyota warned investors that US tariffs represented a significant headwind to its profit targets for the next fiscal year."
2. "Japan's aging population remains a structural headwind for domestic consumption growth, even as wages rise."
3. "We should flag the rising yen as a potential headwind when we present the export forecast to management next week."

**Why it matters:** You will see this word constantly in financial reports, earnings calls, and economic analysis. Its opposite — "tailwind" — means a force that helps rather than hinders. Both terms appear in English-language business media every day, and using them correctly will make your writing and speech sound more natural and professional.
