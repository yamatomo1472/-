Japan, as one of America's closest allies and largest trade partners, has not been spared. The tariff story began in earnest in early 2025, when the U.S. government under President Donald Trump announced a broad set of trade measures aimed at reducing America's trade deficit and bringing manufacturing back home. Toyota, Honda, Nissan, and Mazda together export hundreds of thousands of vehicles to the U.S. each year.

Toyota, Japan's largest company by revenue, responded quickly. The automaker announced it would increase local production at its U.S. plants in Kentucky and Texas. **How to Read a Yield Curve — And Why It Matters**

When financial news talks about the "yield curve," it means the relationship between short-term and long-term interest rates on government bonds. Normally, long-term bonds pay more than short-term ones because lenders want extra reward for waiting longer. When short-term rates are higher than long-term rates, economists call it an "inverted yield curve" — and historically, this has often appeared before a recession. As of early 2026, the U.S. yield curve has moved back toward a normal shape after being inverted for much of 2023 and 2024. Watching the yield curve helps you understand where professional investors think the economy is heading.

## 📖 Word of the Day

**Tariff**

*Noun | /ˈtær.ɪf/ | Origin: from the Arabic word "ta'rif," meaning notification or list of fees, which passed into Spanish and then into English in the late 16th century*

**Definition:** A tax that a government places on goods imported from another country. Tariffs make foreign goods more expensive and are often used to protect local industries or to apply pressure in trade negotiations.
